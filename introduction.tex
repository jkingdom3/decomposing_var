\section{Introduction}

Under Solvency UK and Solvency II, firms are required to hold capital (the
Solvency Capital Requirement, or SCR) which ensures that their basic own funds
are positive with a 99.5\% probability over a one-year horizon. Irrespective of
this, firms may also choose to manage their business under a one-year value-at-
risk ("VaR") setting, or indeed a different horizon.

This brief note outlines a general framework for modelling risk in a general
VaR framework and defines the different sources of risk with mathematical
clarity under such a setting. The concepts that we introduce are not
necessarily new. Our aim however is to remove ambiguity and to recommend a
common framework under which which, ignoring any operational constraints,
all risk specialisms and actuarial modelling teams inside insurance companies
can operate. In addition, this framework can be applied more generically to
firms outside the insurance industry.

The rest of the paper continues as follows. In Section
\ref{section:general_description}, we give a general description of the
environment; in Section 3, we define the risks excluding model risk; in
Section 4, we define model risk and in Section 5, we provide examples of how
this framework applies to various product types.
